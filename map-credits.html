Ensemble mean and spread

GEFS ensemble mean and standard-deviation grids are from NOAA/NCEP GEFS (30 perturbed members in the published products), at native 0.5° resolution. ECMWF IFS ENS mean and standard-deviation grids (51 contributing members) are from ECMWF Open Data, at native 0.25° resolution, under CC BY 4.0. Source data accessed from the newest published run shown on each map, including available hours while the run is still arriving. Snowline converts units and compresses the native global grids for display; it does not calculate substitute ensemble statistics. These maps are not official NOAA or ECMWF products. Mean temperature is converted from kelvin to Celsius; temperature spread retains the equivalent degree interval. Pressure mean and spread are converted from pascals to hPa. No member data is mixed across runs.

MOGREPS-UK precipitation and snowfall probability

MOGREPS-UK (2 km, 3 members per hourly run) precipitation probability comes from Open-Meteo’s Met Office ensemble feed. It counts members reaching at least 0.1 mm/h of total precipitation water equivalent, averaged over each native interval.

Snow risk is calculated by Snowline from the Met Office’s public MOGREPS-UK member snowfall files. A member counts when snowfall reaches at least 0.1 mm/h of liquid-water equivalent averaged over the file’s actual accumulation bounds (0.1 mm in one hour). This is the raw member fraction for falling snow, not a calibrated probability, a settling-snow forecast or a snow-depth estimate. No fixed temperature thresholds, time-lagging or neighbourhood smoothing are used. Incomplete-member cells remain missing.

UK snow probabilities are reprojected by nearest cell from the provider’s ellipsoidal LAEA grid onto the renderer’s spherical 2 km LAEA grid. Display interpolation can show intermediate percentages. Precipitation and snow charts always share the displayed initialization; a newer cycle can await snow processing. Only verified frames are exposed, with their true accumulation periods, and three generated cycles are retained for comparison. UKMO data and Snowline’s derived probabilities are © Crown copyright 2026, Met Office, under CC BY-SA 4.0. Source access is refreshed by the scheduled publisher; the map states the model run used. These are Snowline products, not official Met Office or Netweather snow-risk charts.

Derived upper-air diagnostics

Snowline calculates 850 hPa wet-bulb potential temperature (theta-w) from temperature and relative humidity using Bolton (1980) equivalent potential temperature and the Davies-Jones (2008) approximation. Values are expressed in degrees Celsius at 1000 hPa, with relative humidity interpreted over liquid water. Calculations are checked against MetPy. This is a derived diagnostic, not an official Met Office modified analysis.

300 and 400 hPa relative vorticity uses native same-time winds, centred differences, actual geographic grid geometry and the spherical metric term. No planetary vorticity is added. A separable Gaussian filter (20 km standard deviation, truncated at three standard deviations) reduces grid-scale noise and striping from rounded source winds. This softens small features and peak values; shading, contours and point readouts all use the same smoothed field. Extra neighbouring data prevent smoothing seams between map tiles; missing data are never filled. Longitude smoothing uses the Mercator latitude limit near the poles. The palette is inspired by the supplied Met Office example, not an exact reproduction of the official colour scale. UKV earth-relative wind speed/direction is converted to eastward/northward components before differentiation on its Lambert azimuthal equal-area grid. Values are in 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹; positive is anticlockwise. Missing neighbours, domain boundaries and poles are left blank. Only models with verified inputs and supported wind conventions expose these layers.

Winter Analysis: type, intensity and precipitation symbols

Type and intensity use the published spatial model files, not an image reconstruction or a point forecast. Each calculation stays on one native surface grid and model run. IFS 0.25° uses its native valid-time precipitation-type code. Other supported models estimate interval type from total precipitation and snowfall water equivalent: snow fraction ≤10% is rain, ≥90% snow, otherwise rain–snow mix. Mixed amounts over an interval do not prove simultaneous sleet. Estimates cannot diagnose freezing rain or hail. Native freezing rain/drizzle and ice pellets retain separate colours; missing data is never treated as dry.

Light to dark blue (rain), green (mix) and pink (snow) represent 0.1–<0.5, 0.5–<2, 2–<5 and ≥5 mm/h, calculated as total water equivalent divided by the native reporting interval. This is interval-average intensity, not an instantaneous rate or snow depth. Categories use nearest native cells, without smoothing. Grey indicates unresolved phase or inconsistent source amounts.

The combined chart adds precipitation symbols on a dense 16-pixel lattice and 4 hPa MSLP isobars to derived 850 hPa theta-w. The lattice samples the nearest native cell and does not increase model resolution. Small dots grow into larger rings as dynamic / large-scale precipitation increases through the same four rate bands. Convective precipitation uses compact downward V shapes for light showers and closed triangles for stronger showers. Both components remain plotted when they coexist. ICON supplies independent large-scale rain, convective rain and total snow (stars); total snowfall cannot be separated by mechanism.

GFS and ECMWF AIFS supply total and convective precipitation. Dynamic precipitation is total minus convective, on the same native grid and interval. Both components include rain and snow, so total snowfall is neither subtracted again nor assigned to a mechanism. ECMWF AIFS uses cp; GFS uses interval-integrated CPRAT. See ECMWF precipitation definitions and the NOAA GRIB definitions. Convection exceeding total by at most 0.11 mm is clipped for provider rounding; missing, negative or larger inconsistencies show total as a ring without diagnosing its mechanism.

Continuous rings show large-scale precipitation where a native split is available, or total precipitation where it is unavailable; triangles indicate supplied convection. UKV and IFS 0.25° currently lack the required convective field in this feed. Native or estimated phase still determines their ring colour, with grey for unresolved phase. Neither CAPE nor precipitation strength is used to invent a mechanism. Rings do not diagnose a front or establish that convection is absent. GFS combines its same-run 0.13° surface and 0.25° upper-air feeds.

Field definitions: Open-Meteo model readers; ECMWF feed documentation. Provider unit conversion, deaccumulation and grid processing precede Snowline’s rendering. These are model-based visualisations and labelled diagnostics, not untouched raw GRIB fields.
